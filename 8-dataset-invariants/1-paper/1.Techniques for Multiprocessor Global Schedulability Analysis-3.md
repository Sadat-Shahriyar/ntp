> Source: Sanjoy Baruah, “Techniques for Multiprocessor Global Schedulability Analysis.”

## 1) Models used in the paper

### Workload model

* **Sporadic tasks**: each task $\tau_i=(C_i,D_i,T_i)$ generates an infinite sequence of jobs; each job has WCET $C_i$, relative deadline $D_i$, and minimum inter-arrival time $T_i$ (period). Constrained/implicit deadlines: $D_i \le T_i$; implicit: $D_i=T_i$. Fully preemptive execution assumed. 
* **Demand Bound Function (DBF)** (used throughout analysis): for interval length $t$,
  $$
  \mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i,t)=\max!\Big(0,;\big(\big\lfloor\frac{t-D_i}{T_i}\big\rfloor+1\big)C_i\Big).
  $$


### Resource model

* **$m$ identical unit-capacity processors**, global platform (migration allowed; a job executes on at most one processor at a time). Partitioned vs global scheduling are contrasted; paper focuses on global. 

### Scheduler

* **Global Earliest Deadline First (EDF)** is the focal algorithm; the paper also discusses **Fixed Job Priority (FJP)** class (e.g., hybrid EDF variants) and contrasts partitioned vs global FJP. 

### Analysis framework

* **Utilization & density metrics**:

  * $u_i=C_i/T_i$, $u_{\text{sum}}(\tau)=\sum u_i$, $u_{\max}(\tau)=\max u_i$.
  * $\delta_i=C_i/D_i$, $\delta_{\text{sum}}(\tau)=\sum \delta_i$, $\delta_{\max}(\tau)=\max \delta_i$. 
* **Load parameter**:
  $$
  \mathrm{LOAD}(\tau);=;\max_{t>0}\frac{\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau}\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i,t)}{t}.
  $$
  (Necessary for EDF: $\mathrm{LOAD}(\tau)\le m$, but not sufficient on globals.) 
* **Prior sufficient tests for global EDF** (summarized and critiqued): density test, Baker [BAK], Bertogna–Cirinei–Lipari [BCL]. 
* **New pseudo-polynomial global-EDF test** (main contribution): bounds carry-in to at most $(m-1)$ tasks; generalizes exact uniprocessor EDF test.

## 2) Constraints / invariants (as in the paper) with symbol meanings

> Below, all equations are copied **exactly** (spacing/notation preserved), then followed by a short legend of symbols.

### (A) Core definitions and necessary/sufficient conditions

1. **DBF** (already above). Symbols: $C_i$ WCET, $D_i$ deadline, $T_i$ period, $t$ interval length, $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ floor. 

2. **LOAD** (already above). Symbols: $\tau$ task set; $m$ processors. Necessary condition for schedulability under global EDF is $\mathrm{LOAD}(\tau)\le m$ but Example 1 shows not sufficient. 

3. **Density test (global EDF sufficient)**:
   $$
   \delta_{\mathrm{sum}}(\tau) \le m - (m - 1),\delta_{\max}(\tau). \tag{1}
   $$
   Symbols: $\delta_{\mathrm{sum}}=\sum C_i/D_i$, $\delta_{\max}=\max C_i/D_i$, $m$ processors. 

### (B) New global-EDF test — setup and bounds

* **Arrival/deadline notations**: consider first missed deadline for a job of $\tau_k$ at $t_d$, with arrival $t_a=t_d-D_k$. Let $t_o$ be the latest time $\le t_a$ when at least one processor is idle. Define $A_k \stackrel{\text{def}}{=} t_a-t_o$. Let $\Gamma_k$ be a set of intervals within $[t_a,t_d)$ of total length $(D_k-C_k)$ during which **all** $m$ processors execute jobs **other** than $\tau_k$’s job. 

4. **Necessary condition for a deadline miss**:
   $$
   \sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I(\tau_i) > m \times (A_k + D_k - C_k). \tag{2}
   $$
   Symbols: $I(\tau_i)$ = total work of $\tau_i$ executed over $[t_o,t_a),\cup,\Gamma_k$ in the EDF schedule; $m$ processors; $A_k, D_k, C_k$ as above. 

5. **No carry-in contribution bound ($i\ne k$ vs $i=k$)**:
   $$
   I1(\tau_i)\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}
   \begin{cases}
   \min(\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k),; A_k + D_k - C_k) & \text{if } i \ne k\
   \min(\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k) - C_k,; A_k) & \text{if } i = k
   \end{cases} \tag{3}
   $$
   Symbols: $I1(\tau_i)$ bound when $\tau_i$ has **no** carry-in at $t_o$; DBF as above. 

6. **Carry-in DBF variant**:
   $$
   \mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i,t)\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}
   \left\lfloor \frac{t}{T_i} \right\rfloor \times C_i + \min(C_i,; t \bmod T_i). \tag{4}
   $$
   Symbols: counts full jobs plus at most $C_i$ from a carry-in window when some job’s deadline is at the interval end. 

7. **Carry-in contribution bound**:
   $$
   I2(\tau_i)\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}
   \begin{cases}
   \min(\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i, A_k + D_k),; A_k + D_k - C_k) & \text{if } i \ne k\
   \min(\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i, A_k + D_k) - C_k,; A_k) & \text{if } i = k
   \end{cases} \tag{5}
   $$
   Symbols: $I2(\tau_i)$ bound when $\tau_i$ **does** have carry-in at $t_o$. 

8. **Difference term**:
   $$
   \mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)\stackrel{\text{def}}{=} I2(\tau_i) - I1(\tau_i). \tag{6}
   $$
   Symbols: extra work due to carry-in vs no carry-in for $\tau_i$. 

9. **At most $(m-1)$ carry-ins ⇒ tightened necessary condition**:
   $$
   \sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I1(\tau_i) ;+;
   \sum_{\substack{\text{the }(m-1)\text{ largest}}} \mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)
   ;>; m(A_k + D_k - C_k). \tag{7}
   $$
   Symbols: only the $(m-1)$ largest carry-in “bumps” can exist at $t_o$. 

10. **Main sufficient condition (Theorem 2)**:
    $$
    \boxed{
    \forall \tau_k\in\tau,, \forall A_k\ge 0:\quad
    \left(\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I1(\tau_i) ;+;
    \sum_{\substack{\text{the }(m-1)\text{ largest}}} \mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)\right)
    ;\le; m(A_k + D_k - C_k)
    } \tag{8}
    $$
    Symbols: as above. If (8) holds for every task and every $A_k\ge 0$, the task set is EDF-schedulable on $m$ processors. 

11. **Finite search bound on $A_k$ (Theorem 3)**:
    $$
    A_k \le
    \frac{C_{\Sigma} - D_k(m - U(\tau )) + \sum_i (T_i - D_i)U_i + mC_k}{m - U(\tau)} \tag{9}
    $$
    where $C_{\Sigma}$ is the sum of the $(m-1)$ largest $C_i$’s and $U_i=C_i/T_i$, $U(\tau)=\sum_i U_i$. Symbols: gives a pseudo-polynomial check set for (8). 

12. **Corollary 1 (exactness on uniprocessor)**: The test in (8) **generalizes** the exact uniprocessor EDF test; when $m=1$, (8) reduces to “cumulative DBF over any interval does not exceed interval length.” 

13. **Corollary 2 (complexity)**: With $U(\tau)<m$, (8) needs checking only at DBF change points up to (9) → **pseudo-polynomial** time.

## 3) Statements of examples, lemmas, theorems, corollaries (verbatim) and proofs/examples (as in the paper)

> I reproduce the relevant parts **exactly as presented** (including “Proof Sketch” where that is the paper’s form). Citations follow each item.

### Example 1 (worst-case arrivals for global vs synchronous)

> *“Consider the task system comprised of the three tasks $\tau_1=(1,1,2)$, $\tau_2=(1,1,3)$, and $\tau_3=(5,6,6)$, executing upon two unit-capacity processors. It may be seen (Figure 1 (a)) that the synchronous arrival sequence is successfully scheduled by EDF to meet all deadlines. However, if task $\tau_1$’s second job were to arrive three, rather than two, time units after the first (Figure 1 (b)), then EDF would miss some deadline over the interval $[0,6]$ (and indeed, no scheduling algorithm can possibly guarantee to meet all deadlines for this job arrival sequence).”* 

### Lemma 1 (global can schedule systems that partitioned cannot)

> **Lemma 1** *There are task systems that are schedulable using global FJP algorithms that partitioned FJP algorithms cannot schedule.*
> **Proof Sketch:** *Here’s an example task system, on 2 processors:*
> $\tau_1=(2,2,3), \ \tau_2=(3,3,4), \ \tau_3=(5,12,12)$
> *Clearly, this system cannot be scheduled using partitioning, since $u_1+u_2$, $u_1+u_3$, and $u_2+u_3$ are each $>1$. To show that a global FJP algorithm can schedule this task system, consider any FJP algorithm which assigns lowest priority to $\tau_3$’s jobs. Since there are two processors, $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$’s jobs are guaranteed to meet all deadlines. Now observe that each job of $\tau_3$ needs to execute for 5 time-slots over an interval of 12 slots… [argument continues as in paper with the two bullet cases].* 

### Lemma 2 (partitioned can schedule systems that global cannot)

> **Lemma 2** *There are task systems that are schedulable using partitioned FJP algorithms that global FJP algorithms cannot schedule.*
> **Proof Sketch:** *Here’s an example task system, on 2 processors:*
> $\tau_1=(2,2,3),\ \tau_2=(3,3,4),\ \tau_3=(4,12,12),\ \tau_4=(3,12,12)$
> *This task system may be partitioned by assigning $\tau_1$ and $\tau_3$ to one processor and the other two tasks to the remaining processor, and scheduling each processor using uniprocessor EDF. To show that no global FJP priority assignment scheme can meet all deadlines, consider the synchronous arrival sequence over the interval $[0,12)$. It may be verified that whichever of $\tau_3$’s and $\tau_4$’s job has lower priority ends up missing its deadline while one processor goes idle over $[11,12)$.*
>

### Theorem 1 (incomparability)

> **Theorem 1** *Global and partitioned FJP scheduling are incomparable.* 

### Theorem 2 (main schedulability condition)

> **Theorem 2** *Task system $\tau$ is EDF-schedulable upon $m$ unit-capacity processors if for all tasks $\tau_k\in\tau$ and all $A_k\ge 0$,*
> $$
> \left(\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I1(\tau_i) + \sum_{\text{the }(m-1)\text{ largest}} \mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)\right) \le m(A_k + D_k - C_k).
> $$
> *where $I1(\tau_i)$ and $\mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)$ are as defined in Equations 3 and 6 respectively.* 

### Corollary 1 (uniprocessor exactness)

> **Corollary 1** *The EDF schedulability test of Theorem 2 is a generalization of the exact uniprocessor EDF schedulability test of [9].*
> *Proof Sketch:* *For $m=1$, there are $(m-1)=0$ tasks that are active at time-instant $t_o$… [reduces to classic DBF ≤ interval condition as stated in the paper].* 

### Theorem 3 (finite $A_k$ bound) and proof

> **Theorem 3** *If Condition 8 is to be violated for any $A_k$, then it is violated for some $A_k$ satisfying the condition below:*
> $$
> A_k \le C_{\Sigma} - D_k(m - U(\tau )) + \sum_i (T_i - D_i)U_i + mC_k \over m - U(\tau)
> $$
> *where $C_{\Sigma}$ denotes the sum of the $(m-1)$ largest $C_i$’s.*
> **Proof:** *It is easily seen that $I1(\tau_i) \le \mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k)$, and $I2(\tau_i) \le \mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k) + C_i$… [algebra as in paper leading to the stated bound].* 

### Corollary 2 (complexity)

> **Corollary 2** *The condition in Theorem 2 above can be tested in time pseudo-polynomial in the task parameters, for all task systems $\tau$ for which $U(\tau)$ is bounded by a constant strictly less than the number of processors $m$.*

## Symbol legend (for quick reference)

* $\tau_i=(C_i,D_i,T_i)$: sporadic task with WCET $C_i$, relative deadline $D_i$, period/min inter-arrival $T_i$.
* $u_i=C_i/T_i$, $U_i=C_i/T_i$, $u_{\text{sum}}$, $u_{\max}$: utilizations; $\delta_i=C_i/D_i$, $\delta_{\text{sum}}$, $\delta_{\max}$: densities.
* $\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i,t)$, $\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i,t)$: demand bound function and carry-in variant.
* $m$: number of identical processors.
* $t_o$: last instant $\le t_a$ with at least one idle processor; $t_a=t_d-D_k$ arrival; $t_d$ deadline miss time.
* $A_k=t_a-t_o$; $\Gamma_k$: intervals of total length $(D_k-C_k)$ where all $m$ CPUs are busy with other jobs.
* $I(\tau_i)$: actual executed work of $\tau_i$ over $[t_o,t_a)\cup\Gamma_k$; $I1(\tau_i)$ and $I2(\tau_i)$: upper bounds (no carry-in vs carry-in); $\mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)=I2-I1$.
* $C_\Sigma$: sum of $(m-1)$ largest $C_i$; $U(\tau)=\sum_i U_i$.
