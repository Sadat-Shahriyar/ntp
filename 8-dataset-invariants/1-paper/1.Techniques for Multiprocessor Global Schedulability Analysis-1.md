# Paper: *Techniques for Multiprocessor Global Schedulability Analysis* — Structured Extraction

## 1) Models, Scheduler, and Analysis Framework

### Workload Model

* **Sporadic task system**: each task $\tau_i=(C_i,D_i,T_i)$ with worst-case execution $C_i$, relative deadline $D_i$, and minimum inter-arrival time $T_i$; generates jobs separated by at least $T_i$, each with execution $C_i$ and deadline $D_i$ after arrival (“scheduling window”). Constrained ($D_i\le T_i$) and implicit-deadline ($D_i=T_i$) systems considered.  

* **Task/system characteristics** used in analysis: utilizations $u_i=C_i/T_i$, $u_{\text{sum}}(\tau)$, $u_{\max}(\tau)$; densities $\delta_i=C_i/D_i$, $\delta_{\text{sum}}(\tau)$, $\delta_{\max}(\tau)$; demand bound function (DBF) and load (defined below).  

### Resource Model

* **$m$ identical unit-capacity processors**, *global* cluster (single cluster containing all processors). Jobs may execute on any processor and may migrate; at any instant, a job executes on at most one processor. Inter-processor communication within the (single) cluster assumed no overhead.  

* **Execution model**: fully preemptive; preemptions incur no cost. 

### Scheduler

* Focus on **global EDF** (Earliest-Deadline-First); EDF assigns higher priority to earlier deadlines. Paper also situates EDF within **fixed job-priority (FJP)** class and discusses relation to FTP/DP.   

### Analysis Framework

* Sufficient schedulability tests based on **DBF**, **utilization/density** bounds, and a new **carry-in aware** workload upper bound that leads to **Condition (8)**. Prior tests summarized (density, [BAK], [BCL]); the paper derives a stronger EDF test (Theorem 2) and a finite **search horizon** for $A_k$ (Theorem 3).

## 2) System Constraints / Invariants (verbatim) with symbol explanations

> **Background equations used throughout**

### (a) Demand Bound Function (DBF) and Load

$$
\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i,t) = \max\left(0,\left(\left\lfloor \frac{t - D_i}{T_i}\right\rfloor + 1\right) C_i\right) \tag{DBF}
$$
**Symbols:** $\tau_i$ task; $C_i$ WCET; $D_i$ deadline; $T_i$ period/min inter-arrival; $t$ interval length; $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ floor. Meaning: maximum cumulative execution of all jobs of $\tau_i$ that both arrive and have deadlines within any interval of length $t$. 

$$
\mathrm{LOAD}(\tau) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \max_{t>0};\frac{\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau}\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i,t)}{t} \tag{LOAD}
$$
**Symbols:** $\tau$ task set; numerator is total DBF over all tasks; denominator is interval length $t$. Meaning: worst average processor demand over any interval. 

### (b) Utilization and Density

* $u_i=C_i/T_i$; $u_{\text{sum}}(\tau)=\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau}u_i$; $u_{\max}(\tau)=\max_{\tau_i\in\tau}u_i$.
* $\delta_i=C_i/D_i$; $\delta_{\text{sum}}(\tau)=\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau}\delta_i$; $\delta_{\max}(\tau)=\max_{\tau_i\in\tau}\delta_i$.
  **Meaning:** long-term CPU demand metrics; density ties demand to deadlines.  

---

> **Known sufficient bound (“density test”)**

$$
\delta_{\text{sum}}(\tau) \le m - (m-1),\delta_{\max}(\tau) \tag{1}
$$
**Symbols:** $m$ processors; $\delta_{\text{sum}}$, $\delta_{\max}$ as above. Meaning: if total density is small enough relative to the largest task density, then EDF is schedulable on $m$ processors (sufficient, not necessary).

> **New test derived in the paper (key construction)**

**Setup for a hypothetical first deadline miss under EDF.** Let a job of $\tau_k$ arrive at $t_a$, miss deadline at $t_d$, with $A_k \overset{\text{def}}{=} t_a - t_o$, where $t_o$ is the latest instant $\le t_a$ with at least one processor idle (see Fig. 2). Define $\Gamma_k$ as a set of intervals of total length $(D_k - C_k)$ within $[t_a,t_d)$ during which all $m$ processors execute jobs other than $\tau_k$’s job.  

**Necessary total-work condition for a miss**
$$
\sum_{\tau_i\in \tau} I(\tau_i) > m \times (A_k + D_k - C_k) \tag{2}
$$
**Symbols:** $I(\tau_i)$ = work of $\tau_i$ executing over $[t_o,t_a)\cup\Gamma_k$ in the EDF schedule; RHS is capacity of $m$ processors over that combined interval length. Meaning: to starve $\tau_k$ of $C_k$ units, the other work must exceed available capacity. 

**Upper bounds without/with carry-in** (piecewise, per task):
$$
I_1(\tau_i)\overset{\text{def}}{=}
\begin{cases}
\min\big(\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k), A_k + D_k - C_k\big) & \text{if } i\ne k \
\min\big(\mathrm{DBF}(\tau_i, A_k + D_k) - C_k, A_k\big) & \text{if } i = k
\end{cases} \tag{3}
$$
**Symbols:** $I_1$ = no carry-in contribution uses standard DBF and interval caps. 

To model **carry-in**, define:
$$
\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i,t)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\Big\lfloor \frac{t}{T_i}\Big\rfloor C_i + \min\big(C_i, t \bmod T_i\big) \tag{4}
$$
**Meaning:** max work over length $t$ if some job has its deadline at the interval end and each job runs as late as possible; includes a (partial) carry-in job. 

Then
$$
I_2(\tau_i)\overset{\text{def}}{=}
\begin{cases}
\min\big(\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i, A_k + D_k), A_k + D_k - C_k\big) & \text{if } i\ne k \
\min\big(\mathrm{DBF}'(\tau_i, A_k + D_k) - C_k, A_k\big) & \text{if } i = k
\end{cases} \tag{5}
$$
**Symbols:** $I_2$ = contribution when $\tau_i$ is active at $t_o$ (has carry-in).  

Define the **carry-in delta**
$$
\mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)\overset{\text{def}}{=} I_2(\tau_i) - I_1(\tau_i) \tag{6}
$$
**Meaning:** extra work if $\tau_i$ has carry-in. 

At most $(m-1)$ tasks can be active at $t_o$, so at most $(m-1)$ tasks can contribute with $I_2$; the rest contribute with $I_1$. Hence the necessary condition (2) is equivalent to:
$$
\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I_1(\tau_i) + \sum_{\text{the }(m-1)\text{ largest}}\mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i) > m(A_k + D_k - C_k) \tag{7}
$$
**Meaning:** workload split into base ($I_1$) plus the worst $(m-1)$ carry-ins ($\mathrm{IDIFF}$). 

> **Main sufficient test (Theorem 2)**

If for **all** tasks $\tau_k\in\tau$ and **all** $A_k\ge 0$ the inequality is **not** satisfied (i.e., the LHS never exceeds the RHS), then no deadline can be missed:

$$
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{\tau_i\in\tau} I_1(\tau_i)\right)
+
\left(\sum_{\text{the }(m-1)\text{ largest}}\mathrm{IDIFF}(\tau_i)\right)
\le
m(A_k + D_k - C_k)
}
\tag{8}
$$

**Meaning:** **Sufficient** schedulability condition for EDF on $m$ unit-capacity processors. For $m=1$, it collapses to the classical uniprocessor EDF DBF test (Corollary 1). **Symbols:** as above; sums are over tasks, with the second sum selecting the $(m-1)$ largest $\mathrm{IDIFF}$. 

> **Finite checking horizon (Theorem 3)**

When searching for a violating $A_k$, it suffices to test up to:
$$
A_k \le 
\frac{C_\Sigma - D_k\big(m - U(\tau)\big) + \sum_i (T_i - D_i)U_i + m C_k}{m - U(\tau)}
\tag{9}
$$
where $C_\Sigma$ is the sum of the $(m-1)$ largest $C_i$, and $U(\tau)=\sum_i U_i$ with $U_i=u_i$. **Meaning:** bounds the $A_k$ search; together with the DBF change points it yields pseudo-polynomial testing time (Corollary 2).

## 3) Short “where/how it’s verified” cheat-sheet

* Use **(1)** for a fast sufficient check (density test). 
* If (1) fails, apply **Condition (8)** with bounds **(3)–(6)**; test $A_k$ up to **(9)** (and at DBF change points) to prove EDF-schedulability.
